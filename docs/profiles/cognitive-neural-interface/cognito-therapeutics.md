# Cognito Therapeutics, Inc.

**Category:** Cognitive Disorders / Neural Interface (Alzheimer's disease) | **Status:** Private | **HQ:** Cambridge, Massachusetts, USA | **Founded:** 2016 (MIT spinout, Tsai Lab)

## Funding & Company Status
Total disclosed funding ≈$269M (Crunchbase/Tracxn). Series B (~$73M), Series B extension ($35M, Jan 2024), oversubscribed Series C reported at $105M (~2025–2026, led by Morningside Ventures, IAG Capital Partners, Starbloom Capital) to fund "launch readiness" ahead of anticipated 2027 market entry. (One Tracxn figure cites Series C at $196M — sources disagree; $105M is consistently reported across BusinessWire/Fierce Biotech/MassDevice.)

## Target Nerve / Mechanism
Non-invasive, non-implanted sensory neuromodulation — synchronized 40 Hz (gamma-frequency) flickering light and clicking sound delivered via goggles + headphones (Spectris™, formerly GammaSense), calibrated to a wearer's EEG, designed to entrain gamma oscillations across visual/auditory cortex and, via network propagation, hippocampal and broader cortical circuits implicated in Alzheimer's.

## Biological Basis
Gamma oscillations (~40 Hz), driven by fast-spiking parvalbumin-positive interneurons, are reduced early in Alzheimer's model mice, before plaque deposition or cognitive decline. Iaccarino et al. (*Nature*, 2016, 540:230–235; correction 2018, doi:10.1038/s41586-018-0351-4) showed optogenetic/non-invasive 40 Hz driving of gamma oscillations reduced amyloid-β levels and induced microglial transformation toward an amyloid-clearing phenotype in mouse visual cortex. Human follow-on (OVERTURE Phase 2, *Journal of Alzheimer's Disease*, 2024) reported combined visual+auditory 40 Hz stimulation was associated with reduced white-matter/myelin atrophy in entorhinal-hippocampal tracts.

## Market Size / Affected Population
~6.9M Americans age 65+ living with Alzheimer's (Alzheimer's Association), projected to reach ~13.8M by 2060. Across 7 major markets, diagnosed prevalent AD cases estimated ~15M in 2025 (US ≈44% share). Global AD market estimates: ~$12.4B (2025) up to ~$38.5B by 2034 at 17.5% CAGR (therapeutics-inclusive); AD diagnostics alone ~$9.94B (2025). Cognito's device-specific addressable market not independently disclosed/verified.

## Technology
(1) Patient wears light-emitting goggles + headphones (Spectris) daily at home, 1 hour/day; (2) delivers synchronized visual/auditory stimulation precisely at 40 Hz; (3) onboard/companion EEG sensor calibrates and confirms gamma-band entrainment; (4) repeated daily sessions over months hypothesized to sustain entrainment, modulate microglial activity, and preserve white-matter/network integrity; (5) usage/EEG/compliance data transmitted for remote monitoring.

## Novelty / Differentiation
Unlike anti-amyloid monoclonal antibodies (lecanemab, donanemab), which require infusions and ARIA monitoring and target only amyloid clearance, Cognito's approach is fully non-invasive with (per company/trial reporting) no reported ARIA-type safety signal, targeting a broader network mechanism (gamma entrainment, microglial modulation, white-matter preservation) rather than amyloid alone. Requires no surgery at all, unlike DBS/RNS.

## Limitations — Practical
Requires strict daily compliance (1 hour/day) over a full year in the pivotal trial — real-world adherence in an elderly/cognitively impaired population is an open question. Unclear at what disease stage benefit is retained. Commercial reimbursement pathway not yet established since device is pre-market.

## Limitations — Theoretical / Readiness
The "gamma entrainment slows Alzheimer's" mechanism, while grounded in a well-cited mouse study and early human imaging/functional signals, has not yet been confirmed by a completed, published Phase 3 (HOPE study) readout. Independent commentary (ALZFORUM) has questioned reproducibility/robustness of the original preclinical amyloid-lowering effect. Long-term durability of benefit unknown. Market/clinical readiness nascent given the anticipated 2027 launch.

## Regulatory & Clinical Status
FDA Breakthrough Device Designation granted January 2021. Pivotal trial: HOPE Study (NCT05637801) — randomized, double-blind, sham-controlled, ~70 US sites, 670 participants, 12-month home-use primary endpoint (ADCS-ADL, MMSE), 12-month open-label extension. Predecessor Phase 2 OVERTURE study (n=76, 6-month, sham-controlled) reported ~78–84% slowing of functional decline vs. sham and 61% reduction in whole-brain atrophy on MRI (company-reported, AD/PD 2023). No FDA marketing authorization yet; targeting market entry ~2027.
