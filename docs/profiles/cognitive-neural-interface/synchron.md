# Synchron, Inc.

**Category:** Cognitive Disorders / Neural Interface (severe paralysis / BCI) | **Status:** Private | **HQ:** Brooklyn, New York, USA (originally Melbourne, Australia) | **Founded:** 2016

## Funding & Company Status
Total disclosed funding ≈$345M across four institutional rounds (as of Nov 2025). Series C — $75M, led by ARCH Venture Partners (Dec 2022), with Bill Gates' Gates Frontier, Jeff Bezos' Bezos Expeditions, Khosla Ventures, Reliance Digital Health, Greenoaks (some reports describe this round, with extensions, reaching ~$110M). Series D — $200M, led by Double Point Ventures, closed November 2025, to fund the 2026 pivotal FDA trial; valuation reported at "nearly $1 billion."

## Target Nerve / Mechanism
Primary motor cortex — region controlling hand/arm movement, adjacent to the superior sagittal sinus. The Stentrode is a 16-channel flexible, self-expanding stent-electrode array delivered endovascularly (via the jugular vein) and deployed inside the superior sagittal sinus, recording local field potentials from the adjacent motor cortex without brain-penetrating or open-skull surgery.

## Biological Basis
Primary motor cortex generates movement-intention-related electrophysiological signals (beta/gamma-band desynchronization/synchronization) decodable even when the corticospinal tract is disconnected from muscle (ALS, spinal cord injury). Oxley et al. (*JAMA Neurology*, 2021, SWITCH study) demonstrated endovascular electrodes can record stable neural signal bandwidth over 12 months without penetrating brain tissue. Follow-on work (IOP Publishing, 2025) reports motor activity in gamma/high-gamma bands recorded via Stentrode in two ALS patients.

## Market Size / Affected Population
ALS: US prevalence ~24,821–31,843 (2017, CDC National ALS Registry, PMC9568617). Broader severe-paralysis population (SCI, brainstem stroke, ALS) is larger, but no single verified aggregated addressable-market figure was found. No independently verified $ market-size estimate for implantable BCI found — the space is nascent/pre-commercial and any estimate would be speculative.

## Technology
(1) Catheter threaded from the jugular vein into the superior sagittal sinus under fluoroscopic guidance; (2) self-expanding Stentrode deployed, pressing 16 electrode contacts against the vessel wall over motor cortex; (3) lead tunneled subcutaneously to an infraclavicular implantable transmitter, avoiding craniotomy; (4) implanted unit wirelessly transmits recorded neural signals to an external receiver/computer; (5) ML decoding algorithms translate motor-cortex signal patterns into digital commands (clicks, cursor control, text input), sometimes paired with eye-tracking for cursor positioning.

## Novelty / Differentiation
Core differentiation vs. Neuralink (intracortical penetrating threads, ~1,024 electrodes), Blackrock Neurotech (Utah array, penetrating), and Precision Neuroscience (non-penetrating surface array requiring craniotomy) is that it requires no brain surgery at all — deployment is a catheter-based endovascular procedure performed by interventional neuroradiologists. Traded off against lower channel count (16 channels) vs. intracortical competitors, potentially limiting fine-grained decoding performance.

## Limitations — Practical
Only 16 recording channels — substantially lower bandwidth than intracortical competitors; demonstrations often pair Stentrode "click" decoding with external eye-tracking for cursor positioning, meaning the device alone doesn't yet provide full independent control. Still requires an interventional procedure with attendant risks (though no device-related serious adverse events, vessel occlusion, or migration reported in trial cohorts to date). Substantial training/calibration burden. Chronic signal stability beyond ~12 months and long-term durability still being characterized in small cohorts (4 SWITCH patients, 6 COMMAND patients).

## Limitations — Theoretical / Readiness
No implantable BCI from any company has yet achieved FDA marketing authorization — Synchron's planned 2026 pivotal trial is the study needed before any PMA submission, so this remains pre-commercial/investigational-only. No US national coverage determination or established reimbursement pathway for implantable BCIs exists yet. Broader theoretical/ethical questions attach to BCI as a category: long-term neural-tissue/vascular interface safety, neural-data privacy/security, patient autonomy/informed consent in a severely paralyzed population, and the risk of benefit being outpaced by hype given very early clinical-stage maturity.

## Regulatory & Clinical Status
FDA Breakthrough Device Designation granted August 2020. SWITCH study (Australia, first-in-human, n=4) — published JAMA Neurology, stable signal bandwidth over 12 months, no persistent neurological deficits, no vessel occlusion/migration. COMMAND study (US, NCT05035823, n=6, 12-month follow-up) — met primary safety endpoint, no device-related serious adverse events; participants performed digital tasks via decoded motor intent. No FDA marketing authorization yet; pivotal trial planned for 2026 to support the first-ever PMA submission for an implantable BCI.
