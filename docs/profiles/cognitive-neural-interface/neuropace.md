# NeuroPace, Inc.

**Category:** Cognitive Disorders / Neural Interface (drug-resistant epilepsy) | **Ticker:** NASDAQ: NPCE | **HQ:** Mountain View, California, USA | **Founded:** 1997

## Funding & Company Status
IPO April 2021: $117.3M gross ($105.5M net, 6.9M shares at $17.00). Market cap ≈$392M (May 2025) and ≈$560.5M (July 2026). June 2025: new $75M credit facility with MidCap Financial ($60M term loan + $15M revolver). FY2024 revenue: $79.9M (up 22% from $65.4M FY2023). Pre-IPO private funding total not separately verified.

## Target Nerve / Mechanism
Direct cortical/subcortical brain targets — the RNS System places 1–2 depth and/or cortical strip leads at the patient's specific seizure focus/foci, connected to a cranially-implanted neurostimulator that continuously records intracranial EEG (iEEG) and delivers closed-loop, responsive stimulation to the seizure-onset zone when an abnormal pattern is detected — a "brain pacemaker," in contrast to open-loop VNS or thalamic DBS.

## Biological Basis
Epilepsy is increasingly understood as a distributed brain network disorder, with pathological synchronization spreading across interconnected cortical/subcortical nodes (Fan et al., PMC, 2022). RNS is believed to work via network-level effects; a 2022 study found patient-specific brain network connectivity predicts RNS effectiveness (Kim et al., PMC9123848). Long-term network reorganization over months to years appears to underlie RNS efficacy growing over time (Khambhati et al., *Science Translational Medicine*, 2021, doi:10.1126/scitranslmed.abf6588).

## Market Size / Affected Population
~30–40% of ~3.4M US epilepsy patients have drug-resistant epilepsy (DRE); global pooled DRE prevalence ~30% (Kalilani et al., PubMed 30426482); ~637,000 US DRE cases (2017 data). Only ~20% of DRE patients are candidates for/willing to undergo resective surgery. NeuroPace (SEC filing, 2023) estimates a ~$22B US addressable opportunity for focal DRE and ~$6B for pediatric/generalized epilepsy. Broader epilepsy device market projected to reach $14.4B globally by 2034 (24.9% CAGR). Over 8,000 patients have received the RNS System since 2014 launch.

## Technology
(1) Presurgical workup localizes 1–2 seizure foci; (2) neurosurgeon implants a small neurostimulator in the skull with leads at the focus/foci; (3) device continuously monitors ECoG/iEEG using patient-specific detection algorithms; (4) on detecting the patient's individual electrographic seizure-onset pattern, delivers brief imperceptible pulses to interrupt it before a clinical seizure develops; (5) data stored/downloaded/uploaded to a cloud platform for physician review and remote re-tuning.

## Novelty / Differentiation
Only FDA-approved closed-loop, responsive brain stimulator for epilepsy — VNS (LivaNova) and thalamic DBS (Medtronic) are open-loop, scheduled/continuous, without real-time seizure detection. Reversible vs. resective surgery, applicable to foci in eloquent cortex or bilateral/multifocal disease. The chronic iEEG dataset it generates is a unique long-term "brain data" asset unmatched by VNS/DBS competitors.

## Limitations — Practical
Requires cranial surgery (craniotomy) with infection/hemorrhage/hardware-erosion risk; battery/device longevity requires eventual replacement surgery; efficacy is gradual (median seizure reduction grows from ~44% at 1 year to higher levels over years); requires specialized epilepsy-center expertise for lead placement and iterative programming; substantial cost; only ~20% of DRE patients are even surgical candidates/willing.

## Limitations — Theoretical / Readiness
The precise network mechanism of benefit remains incompletely understood — no validated biomarker prospectively predicts responders, so selection remains largely empiric. Long-term (decades-scale) hardware durability still being characterized via post-approval studies. Expansion to idiopathic generalized epilepsy is not yet approved — FDA responded that NeuroPace's 2025 PMA supplement for IGE (based on NAUTILUS trial) was "not approvable in its current form," unresolved as of mid-2026.

## Regulatory & Clinical Status
Original PMA approval November 14, 2013, for adults ≥18 with partial (focal) seizures from ≤2 foci refractory to ≥2 anti-epileptic drugs. Pivotal trial: randomized, sham-controlled (n=191) — 37.9% median seizure reduction (active) vs. 17.3% (sham), p=0.012; long-term follow-up showed 44%/53% median reduction at 1/2 years. FDA Breakthrough Device Designation for IGE indication; pivotal NAUTILUS trial reported 77% median GTC seizure reduction at 18 months (company release), but the subsequent PMA supplement was deemed not approvable by FDA as of 2025.
