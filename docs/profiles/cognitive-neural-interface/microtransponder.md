## Clinical Indication

**Disorder Category:** Cognitive Disorders / Neural Interface (post-stroke motor rehabilitation)
**Primary Indication:** Chronic (typically ≥6 months to years post-injury) upper-limb motor deficit following ischemic stroke.

---

## Target Nerve or Mechanism

**Anatomical Target:** Cervical vagus nerve
**Delivery Mechanism:** An implanted pulse generator (similar form factor to epilepsy VNS devices), with a lead cuffed around the cervical vagus nerve, delivers a brief stimulation pulse time-locked ("paired") with high-repetition physical/occupational therapy movements, reinforcing neuroplastic strengthening of the motor circuits engaged during the practiced movement. Stimulation is triggered by the patient/clinician via a handheld device during scheduled in-clinic and home therapy sessions ([Vivistim](https://www.vivistim.com/about/)).
**Biological basis:** VNS time-locked to movement is proposed to engage cholinergic/noradrenergic nucleus-basalis-dependent cortical map plasticity ([PMC8568982](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8568982/); [PubMed 24309259](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24309259/)). The pivotal clinical evidence is the randomized, blinded, sham-controlled **VNS-REHAB trial**: Dawson et al., "Vagus nerve stimulation paired with rehabilitation for upper limb motor function after ischaemic stroke (VNS-REHAB): a randomised, blinded, pivotal, device trial," *The Lancet*, 2021 ([PubMed 33894832](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33894832/)).

## Regulatory Status & Clinical Trial

- **FDA Breakthrough Device Designation**, followed by **PMA approval (P210007)** on **August 27, 2021**, for chronic ischemic-stroke-related upper-limb motor deficits ([NeurologyLive](https://www.neurologylive.com/view/fda-approves-vivistim-vns-system-ischemic-stroke-motor-deficits); corroborated by [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/02/14/2024-03025/determination-of-regulatory-review-period-for-purposes-of-patent-extension-vivistim-system)).
- **Pivotal trial: VNS-REHAB** ([NCT03131960](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03131960)) — 108 patients (53 active / 55 sham) across 19 US/UK sites, enrolled Oct 2017–Sep 2019, published in *The Lancet* 2021 ([PubMed 33894832](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33894832/)).
- Earlier feasibility trial: [NCT01669161](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01669161).
- Post-market registry: **GRASP Vivistim Registry** ([NCT05301140](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05301140)).
- Newer trial: "Repair Study" ([NCT06722677](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06722677)).

## Novelty & Differentiation

First and only FDA-approved device-based intervention specifically for chronic post-stroke upper-limb motor deficits; most rehabilitation competitors are non-invasive (tDCS/TMS) or purely behavioral. Company-cited pivotal-trial data claims 2–3x greater arm/hand function gains versus therapy alone ([NeurologyLive](https://www.neurologylive.com/view/fda-approves-vivistim-vns-system-ischemic-stroke-motor-deficits)).

---

## Limitations: Practical & Theoretical

- Requires an invasive surgical implantation procedure with associated surgical/anesthesia risk and cost.
- Requires ongoing in-clinic, therapist-supervised sessions to pair stimulation with movement — an adherence and access burden.
- Durability of benefit and mechanism specificity beyond arm/hand function are not yet established for other stroke deficits ([Vivistim](https://www.vivistim.com/about/); trial design: [PubMed 31903435](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31903435/)).
- Two-year follow-up data exist, but longer-term real-world outcomes are still accumulating ([PMC13382849](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13382849/)).
- Exact cumulative lifetime funding total could not be reconciled precisely across sources — **[unverified]**.

---

## Market Size

Upper-limb weakness affects an estimated **35–70%** of stroke survivors, depending on study era and methodology (35% at ~1 day post-stroke in recent data vs. ~70% in the older 1990s Copenhagen Stroke Study) ([PMC10878009](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10878009/); [SAGE Journals](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/15459683241229676)).
