# ONWARD Medical N.V. (ARC-EX / ARC-IM)

**Category:** Neuro-Musculoskeletal / Pain / Movement (spinal cord injury) | **Ticker:** Euronext Brussels & Amsterdam: ONWD | **HQ:** Eindhoven, Netherlands | **Founded:** 2014 (as G-Therapeutics/GTX Medical, an EPFL spinoff)

## Funding & Company Status
Originated from Prof. Grégoire Courtine's lab (EPFL); merged 2019 with NeuroRecovery Technologies, rebranded ONWARD Medical. Dual-listed on Euronext Brussels and Euronext Amsterdam since October 2021 (IPO priced at €12.75/share, ~€380M market cap at listing). As of Sept 2026, market cap ≈€180.4 million — substantial de-rating since IPO. Follow-on raises: ~$47.2M private placement; ~€50.85M capital increase announced/closed October 22–24, 2025. Precise lifetime cumulative funding total not reconciled into a single figure — in the hundreds of millions of euros across venture rounds + IPO + follow-ons.

## Target Nerve / Mechanism
Targets lumbosacral and cervical spinal cord circuitry below/around the level of injury. Two platforms: **ARC-EX** is non-invasive, transcutaneous electrical spinal cord stimulation (surface electrodes over cervical spine) during rehab exercise or at home; **ARC-IM** is an implantable epidural spinal cord stimulation platform delivering targeted, spatiotemporally patterned stimulation timed to intended movement.

## Biological Basis
Foundational science: Wagner FB, Mignardot JB, et al., "Targeted neurotechnology restores walking in humans with spinal cord injury," *Nature*, 2018;563(7729):65-71 (three participants with chronic, motor-incomplete SCI regained voluntary stepping control). Follow-on: "Activity-dependent spinal cord neuromodulation rapidly restores trunk and leg motor functions after complete paralysis," *Nature Medicine*, 2021. For ARC-EX specifically: "Non-invasive spinal cord electrical stimulation for arm and hand function in chronic tetraplegia," *Nature Medicine*, 2024, PubMed 38769431.

## Market Size / Affected Population
US annual traumatic SCI incidence ~54 cases/million (~18,421 new cases/year); US prevalence ~311,560 people living with traumatic SCI (range 261,168–399,079) [NSCISC data via Reeve Foundation, UAB NSCISC]. No independently verified global prevalence figure or SCI-neurotech-specific dollar market-size estimate found.

## Technology
**ARC-EX (cleared/marketed):** surface electrodes over cervical spine skin; wearable external pulse generator delivers programmed transcutaneous stimulation; used concurrently with structured rehab exercises (~25 sessions in the Up-LIFT trial), and as of November 2025's 510(k) expansion, at home. **ARC-IM (investigational):** electrode array surgically implanted in the epidural space connected to an IPG; spatiotemporally patterned stimulation timed to attempted movement (sometimes BCI-triggered in research settings); also studied for cardiovascular/blood-pressure regulation after SCI.

## Novelty / Differentiation
Distinct from Nevro/Neuros/SetPoint in aiming for restorative/functional gain rather than symptom suppression or immune modulation. ARC-EX's non-invasive delivery differentiates it from the implanted-electrode SCI research paradigm, lowering the barrier for a first commercial product. First FDA-authorized (De Novo) non-invasive spinal cord stimulation system for SCI — a genuinely novel regulatory category.

## Limitations — Practical
ARC-EX requires repeated, supervised rehabilitation sessions (~25 average in Up-LIFT) — time- and resource-intensive for patients and clinics. ARC-IM (the more powerful, implantable platform) still requires surgical implantation and remains investigational (not yet FDA-cleared/approved). Commercial revenue is still nascent: ARC-EX system revenue grew from just €0.08M (2024) to €3.7M (2025). CPT coding/reimbursement details not found in available sources.

## Limitations — Theoretical / Readiness
The least mature/most research-stage company of the four in terms of the "restoration" claim: Up-LIFT showed statistically significant improvements (72% exceeded minimally important difference thresholds on strength and function; 90% experienced gains, 87% reported improved quality of life), but this is improvement in existing residual capacity in incomplete SCI patients using a non-invasive, adjunct-to-rehabilitation device — not full "cure." The more dramatic "walking again" results (Wagner 2018, Nature Medicine 2021/2023) come from small academic cohorts using the still-investigational implantable ARC-IM/BCI-linked systems, not yet commercially available or FDA-approved — a real gap between attention-grabbing academic headlines and currently regulatory-cleared, purchasable reality that diligence should carefully separate.

## Regulatory & Clinical Status
**ARC-EX:** FDA De Novo classification and market authorization granted December 19, 2024 — first FDA-authorized non-invasive SCS system for SCI, for adults with chronic, non-progressive impairment from incomplete cervical SCI to improve hand strength and sensation. FDA 510(k) clearance for expanded home-use indication granted November 17, 2025. CE Mark obtained for EU commercial launch. Pivotal trial: Up-LIFT (NCT04697472) — 65 tetraplegic participants enrolled, 60 completed; after ~25 average sessions, 72% showed improvement exceeding minimally-important-difference thresholds; published *Nature Medicine*, May 2024; no related serious adverse events. **ARC-IM:** still investigational, not FDA-cleared/approved as of this research.
