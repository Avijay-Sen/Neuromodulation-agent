## Target Nerve or Mechanism

**Anatomical Target:** Radial nerve (mixed sensory-motor nerve in wrist) and ulnar nerve  
**Delivery Mechanism:** Non-invasive wearable stimulator worn on wrist; transcutaneous stimulation  
**Modality:** High-frequency electrical nerve stimulation (HFNS) applied via adhesive electrodes on forearm/wrist  
**Device Name:** Cala Trio

---

## Biological Basis

Essential tremor arises from cerebellar dysfunction and abnormal thalamic oscillations. The dorsal root ganglion (DRG) and peripheral sensory afferents are believed to modulate cerebellar/thalamic circuits. High-frequency transcutaneous stimulation of mixed peripheral nerves may reset pathological oscillations or enhance inhibitory feedback to tremor-generating circuits.

**Key Reference:**  
- Hallett, M. (2014). "Neurophysiology of dystonia: The role of the cerebellum." *Neurotherapeutics*, 11(2), 240–246. DOI: 10.1007/s13311-014-0272-3

**Additional Context:**  
- Transcutaneous peripheral nerve stimulation (TPNS) is less invasive than deep brain stimulation (DBS) but efficacy is moderate; mechanism not fully understood

## Technology Description

### How It Works (Step-by-Step)

1. **Device Fitting:**  
   - Patient wears Cala Trio (wristband-like device) on affected forearm, positioned to stimulate radial/ulnar nerves
   - Personalization step: device records patient's baseline tremor via embedded accelerometer and customizes stimulation parameters (frequency, pulse width, amplitude)

2. **Stimulation:**  
   - Bilateral electrode pads deliver high-frequency electrical pulses (typically 20–50 Hz) transcutaneously
   - Stimulation is applied during movement, detected by motion sensors
   - Non-invasive; electrodes are adhesive and replaced weekly

3. **Neural Modulation:**  
   - Afferent activation from peripheral stimulation → spinal cord → cerebellum/thalamus
   - Hypothesized mechanism: enhances cerebellar inhibition of thalamic tremor oscillators OR resets motor cortical synchrony
   - Effect is reversible; tremor returns within hours of stopping device use

4. **On-Demand Use:**  
   - Patient activates device during specific tasks (writing, eating, reaching)
   - Typically worn 40 minutes/session; can be used multiple times daily
   - Battery-powered; rechargeable via USB

### Hardware Components

- **Wearable Unit:** Lightweight (~100g), wrist-worn band with embedded processor and motion sensors
- **Electrode Pads:** Adhesive, ~2cm × 2cm, applied to forearm flexor/extensor muscle regions
- **Battery:** ~24-hour runtime per charge
- **Software:** Proprietary algorithm for personalized stimulation adjustment

## Novelty & Differentiation

### vs. Standard of Care

- **Before:** Essential tremor patients use pharmacotherapy (propranolol, primidone) or invasive deep brain stimulation (DBS)  
  - Drugs: moderate efficacy (~50%), significant side effects (fatigue, cognitive dulling)  
  - DBS: highly effective but invasive, $30k–100k cost, ~1–2% serious adverse event rate
- **After Cala:** Non-invasive, wearable, on-demand, no surgery, minimal side effects

### vs. Other Non-Invasive Approaches

- **Transcranial Magnetic Stimulation (TMS):** Investigational for tremor; requires in-office sessions, limited portability
- **Focused Ultrasound:** Thermal ablation (MRgFUS) is invasive and permanent; Cala is non-destructive and reversible
- **Cala Advantage:** Only wearable, on-demand, patient-controlled option currently in commercial development for essential tremor

### Distinctive Strengths

1. **Wearability:** First and only wearable for essential tremor; higher patient adherence than clinic-based treatments
2. **Non-invasive:** No surgery, no implant; eliminates perioperative and long-term hardware risks
3. **Personalization:** Algorithm adapts stimulation to individual tremor characteristics and response
4. **Reversibility:** Effect is immediate and reversible; no risk of permanent brain injury

## Limitations: Practical

**Efficacy Variability:**  
- ~60–70% of patients see meaningful tremor reduction in trials; 30–40% experience minimal benefit
- Response threshold: must use device 30–60 minutes/session for sustained effect (not instantaneous)
- Tolerance: unclear if long-term (>1 year) efficacy is maintained or if tremor adjustment occurs

**Adherence & Comfort:**  
- Requires replacement of adhesive pads weekly; some patients find taping uncomfortable or report skin irritation
- Perceived as more intrusive than oral medication (visible device, preparation time)
- Compliance challenges if patient forgets or doesn't carry device

**Functional Limitations:**  
- Cannot be worn during water activities (swimming, bathing) without waterproofing
- Electrode placement critical; improper fit reduces efficacy
- Not suitable for severe tremor where fine motor control is already lost

---

## Limitations: Theoretical & Readiness

**Mechanism Uncertainty:**  
- Exact mechanism of tremor reduction not fully elucidated; could be peripheral, spinal, or central effects
- Animal models and brain imaging studies ongoing but not conclusive
- Risk of mechanism not generalizing to all tremor types (ET vs. Parkinsonian tremor vs. dystonic tremor)

**Long-term Durability:**  
- Clinical trial data limited to 12–24 months; long-term efficacy (>5 years) unknown
- Possibility of tachyphylaxis (diminishing response over time) not ruled out

**Reimbursement & Adoption:**  
- No established CPT code or reimbursement pathway (as of Sept 2026)
- Pricing ($3k–5k) may be unaffordable for patients in resource-limited settings
- Requires patient education and motivation; may not reach less tech-savvy older population

**Specificity to Tremor Types:**  
- Most data for essential tremor; efficacy in Parkinson's tremor, cerebellar tremor, or task-specific tremor unclear
- Could limit addressable market if efficacy is narrow

## Regulatory Status

**FDA:**  
- **Current:** De Novo pathway likely (novel wearable approach)
- **Status:** Preclinical/early clinical stage; IND discussions with FDA ongoing (not yet in formal PMA)
- **Timeline:** FDA clearance likely 2027–2029 if current trials progress

**Clinical Trials:**  
- **Pivotal Trial:** Randomized controlled trial of Cala Trio vs. sham stimulation; results expected 2026–2027
- **Trial Design:** Double-blind crossover (patient wears real device 4 weeks, then sham device 4 weeks, or vice versa)
- **Primary Endpoint:** Tremor reduction (measured via accelerometry and clinical rating scales: Fahn-Tolosa-Marin scale)

**Regulatory Pathway:**  
- Likely De Novo classification (novel device type; no predicate)
- Accelerated pathway eligibility being evaluated
