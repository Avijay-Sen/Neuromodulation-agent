# Neuros Medical, Inc. (Altius System)

**Category:** Neuro-Musculoskeletal / Pain / Movement (post-amputation neuropathic pain) | **Status:** Private | **HQ:** Willoughby (Willoughby Hills), Ohio, USA (some sources also list Aliso Viejo, CA) | **Founded:** 2008

## Funding & Company Status
Founded by Jon Snyder. Estimates vary by aggregator: Tracxn ~$145M over 14 rounds; CB Insights ~$224.85M; Startup Intros ~$147.2M over 8 rounds (not fully reconcilable — treat as approximate). Most recently verified: $56M Series D, announced July 9, 2025, earmarked to commercialize the newly FDA-approved Altius System. Earlier verified: $38.5M Series BB, announced January 2021, to fund completion of the QUEST pivotal trial.

## Target Nerve / Mechanism
Direct stimulation of the peripheral nerve(s) proximal to the amputation site (e.g., sciatic nerve) via a surgically implanted nerve cuff electrode connected to an implantable pulse generator. Peripheral (not central/spinal) target. Delivers high-frequency alternating current (HFAC) to induce a reversible, on-demand conduction block, physically preventing aberrant pain signals from neuroma/injured nerve sites from reaching the spinal cord/brain — fundamentally different mechanism from SCS's gate-control paradigm.

## Biological Basis
Post-amputation pain arises from peripheral and central mechanisms. Peripherally, transected nerve endings form neuromas with upregulated sodium channels, downregulated potassium channels, ectopic electrical activity, and hypersensitivity [Springer 2024; tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.2147/JPR.S32299]. This peripheral barrage contributes to central sensitization (spinal dorsal horn "wind-up," NMDA-mediated) and phantom limb pain via cortical reorganization [PMC11876430, 2025]. Neuros Medical's rationale: blocking peripheral nerve conduction directly at/near the neuroma interrupts the pathological signal at its anatomical source.

## Market Size / Affected Population
~185,000 US amputations annually (82% vascular disease, 16.4% trauma, <1% cancer/congenital); ~2.3 million people living with limb loss (2005 baseline of 1.6M projected to reach 3.6M by 2050). Global prevalence of phantom limb pain among amputees ~64%, published incidence estimates 50–95% depending on study/definition [PubMed 39234405; PMC11371778; PubMed 34583023]. No independent market-size-in-dollars estimate found; existing treatment dominated by opioids/gabapentinoids, which Neuros positions its device to displace.

## Technology
(1) Cuff electrode surgically implanted around the target peripheral nerve (e.g., sciatic) proximal to amputation; (2) connects to a subcutaneously implanted IPG; (3) patient-controlled and on-demand — patient activates via external controller during pain flares rather than continuous stimulation; (4) delivers high-frequency alternating current inducing a temporary, reversible nerve conduction block without paresthesia.

## Novelty / Differentiation
Mechanistically distinct from every other company in this cohort and mainstream SCS competitors (Medtronic, Boston Scientific, Abbott) — targets the peripheral nerve directly with a conduction-block mechanism, and is on-demand/patient-triggered rather than continuously delivered. Received FDA Breakthrough Device Designation (2021) for a "novel" high-frequency nerve block approach; pivotal QUEST trial demonstrated superiority over active-sham control (a genuinely blinded comparator, rare in device trials).

## Limitations — Practical
Requires surgical implantation of a peripheral nerve cuff electrode (invasive, with surgical/infection/nerve-injury risks; device-specific complication rates not independently verified). Patient education/compliance in operating the external controller required. As a newly approved device (2024) with a single early commercial implant site reported (Baylor Scott & White, Dallas) at launch, near-term surgeon training and site availability are real constraints. MRI compatibility, battery-life/recharge cadence, and pricing/reimbursement not found in available sources.

## Limitations — Theoretical / Readiness
As a first-in-category "high-frequency nerve block" mechanism, long-term durability and comparative effectiveness versus targeted muscle reinnervation (TMR) or regenerative peripheral nerve interface (RPNI) surgical alternatives [PMC13240872] have not been established over long follow-up in independent literature. Very newly commercial (approved Aug 2024, first commercial implants ~late 2024/2025) — real-world post-market outcomes, payer coverage maturity, and broad surgeon adoption remain early-stage/unproven.

## Regulatory & Clinical Status
FDA Breakthrough Device Designation received 2021. FDA PMA (P230020) filed June 30, 2023, approved August 26, 2024, indicated as "an aid in the management of chronic intractable phantom and residual lower limb post-amputation pain in adult amputees." Pivotal trial: QUEST (NCT02221934) — 180-subject, randomized, double-blinded, active-sham-controlled IDE study; all primary efficacy and safety endpoints met, demonstrating superiority over active-sham control, with statistically significant reductions in pain intensity, decreased opioid/pain-medication use, and quality-of-life improvements. First commercial implant announced following FDA approval.
