# NeuroSigma, Inc.

**Category:** Mental Health / Psychiatric & Neurodevelopmental (pediatric ADHD) | **Status:** Private | **HQ:** Los Angeles, California, USA | **Founded:** 2008

## Funding & Company Status
Founded by Leon Ekchian; core technology licensed exclusively from UCLA. Total disclosed funding reported ~$17.9 million (CB Insights), though a separate Tracxn snapshot reported only ~$1.71M — figures conflict significantly and could not be reconciled; treat as roughly high-single-digit to ~$18M. Disclosed funders include NIH/NINDS grants, UCLA, KidsX, Checkmate Capital. No IPO; remains private. A "pilot program" reportedly dispensed 1,000+ Monarch devices and 100,000+ disposable patches; CEO cited a waitlist of 1,500+ ahead of the July 2026 second-generation US commercial launch.

## Target Nerve / Mechanism
External trigeminal nerve stimulation (eTNS) — an adhesive electrode patch on the forehead (targeting the ophthalmic/V1 branch of the trigeminal nerve bilaterally), delivering low-level electrical pulses nightly during sleep. Proposed mechanism: afferent trigeminal signals project to brainstem nuclei (e.g., locus coeruleus, nucleus tractus solitarius) that modulate frontal-striatal and limbic circuits implicated in ADHD's noradrenergic/dopaminergic dysregulation; company materials have cited fMRI sub-study data suggesting increased frontal brain activity with treatment, though a full peer-reviewed neuroimaging paper for that specific claim could not be independently verified.

## Biological Basis — Including a Material Evidence Update
Pivotal trial: McGough et al., 2019, *Journal of the American Academy of Child & Adolescent Psychiatry* 58(4):403–411 — 62 unmedicated children (ages 8–12) randomized to active (n=32) or sham (n=30) TNS; after 4 weeks, ADHD-RS scores fell from 34.1→23.4 (active) vs. 33.7→27.5 (sham), p=0.005, Cohen's d=0.5.

**Critical update for diligence:** a much larger, adequately powered confirmatory Phase 2b trial ("ATTENS," ISRCTN82129325; n=150, mean age 12.6), published in *Nature Medicine*, January 16, 2026, found **no statistically significant clinical benefit** of active eTNS over sham on ADHD symptoms or related cognitive measures, despite good adherence and a favorable safety profile. Medical press (covering the study's lead investigator) reported: *"This treatment is very safe, but it's not working for ADHD... My message to psychiatrists is not to use this treatment and to families not to buy the device,"* and explicitly called for FDA to revisit the device's clearance. The original 2019 clearance rested on a single small (n=62), short (4-week) trial that a larger, better-powered, independent follow-up failed to replicate — a material, load-bearing evidence-base risk.

## Market Size / Affected Population
US pediatric ADHD prevalence: ~11.4% of US children (~7.1 million) ever diagnosed, ~10.5% (~6.5 million) current ADHD (2022 NSCH data, PubMed 38778436); ~53.6% (~3.4 million) of those with current ADHD take medication, implying ~3 million unmedicated children in the FDA-cleared target demographic (ages 7–12, monotherapy, not currently on prescription ADHD medication). US ADHD therapeutics market ~$5.47 billion (2025); global ~$14.96 billion (2025) growing to $23.89 billion by 2035 — overwhelmingly pharmaceutical-dominated (medication ~65% share), not a device-specific TAM.

## Technology
Monarch eTNS System — a small controller wired to a single-use adhesive forehead patch, used at home nightly during sleep, prescription-only under caregiver supervision. First-generation FDA-cleared 2019. Second-generation ("Monarch 2.0") — ~1/3 the size, color LCD screen, improved battery/UI — received FDA 510(k) clearance January 17, 2024 and began broader US commercial launch mid-2026.

## Novelty / Differentiation
The first (and to date, only) FDA-cleared non-drug treatment for pediatric ADHD — positioned for parents/clinicians seeking to avoid or delay stimulant medication in young children. Fully at-home use (unlike TMS devices) with no clinic visits required after prescription/setup. Distinguishes itself from the other three mental-health companies by being the only pediatric-indicated device and the only one targeting a peripheral cranial nerve rather than direct cortical stimulation.

## Limitations — Practical
Requires nightly wear during sleep for at least 4 weeks (compliance burden in children); recurring cost of single-use disposable patches; narrow FDA label (only unmedicated children ages 7–12, monotherapy) excludes medicated patients, adolescents, and adults; US insurance/payer coverage not clearly established — largely appears to be a cash-pay/limited-coverage model at this stage.

## Limitations — Theoretical / Readiness
This is the most significant readiness concern of any company profiled to date: the trigeminal-brainstem-limbic mechanistic hypothesis remains largely inferential, and — critically — the January 2026 Nature Medicine confirmatory Phase 2b trial found no significant clinical benefit versus sham in a well-powered, independent replication, prompting explicit public calls to reconsider the FDA clearance. This creates a substantial gap between the original (thin) evidence base that supported 2019 clearance and current best evidence, and represents a major flag for any VC assessing the durability of NeuroSigma's regulatory/clinical moat, reimbursement prospects, and long-term commercial viability.

## Regulatory & Clinical Status
FDA De Novo clearance April 19, 2019 (first-generation Monarch, the first-ever FDA clearance of a non-drug treatment for pediatric ADHD); second-generation device cleared via 510(k) January 17, 2024. Key trials: pivotal NCT02155608 (McGough et al. 2019, JAACAP); confirmatory Phase 2b "ATTENS" trial (ISRCTN82129325; results in Nature Medicine, Jan. 16, 2026) — **headline result: no significant benefit vs. sham**, a finding that directly contradicts the evidentiary basis for the original clearance and is now publicly driving calls for FDA reconsideration.
