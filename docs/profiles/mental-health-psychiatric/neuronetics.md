# Neuronetics, Inc. (NeuroStar)

**Category:** Mental Health / Psychiatric (MDD, anxious depression, adjunct OCD, adolescent MDD) | **Ticker:** NASDAQ: STIM | **HQ:** Malvern, Pennsylvania, USA | **Founded:** 2003

## Funding & Company Status
IPO'd on NASDAQ June 2018, raising $107.5M (6,325,000 shares at $17/share); had raised ~$187M in private venture funding prior (~$294.5M lifetime capital as of 2018). Post-IPO: $10M from Perceptive Advisors (Q4 2024); secondary offering raising ~$18.9M net (Feb 2025). FY2024 revenue: $74.9M (+5% YoY). Q1 2025 revenue: $32.0M (+84% YoY, Greenbrook acquisition). Q2 2026 revenue: $41.6M (+9.1% YoY). Market cap ~$170–224M (micro-cap). Aug 2024: agreed to acquire competitor Greenbrook TMS Inc. (all-stock, closed Dec 10, 2024); targeting $21M annualized cost synergies.

## Target Nerve / Mechanism
Figure-8 coil positioned over the left dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (DLPFC), delivering high-frequency (10 Hz) repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation (rTMS) pulses that induce focal electric currents depolarizing underlying cortical neurons and propagating to functionally connected deeper structures.

## Biological Basis
Major depression is associated with relative hypoactivity of the left DLPFC and relative hyperactivity of the right DLPFC, which high-frequency rTMS is thought to rebalance. Pivotal trial: O'Reardon et al., 2007, *Biological Psychiatry* 62(11):1208–1216 — 301 antidepressant-free, treatment-resistant MDD patients, active TMS safe/effective vs. sham. A systematic review (PubMed 26349810) concludes rTMS engages a corticolimbic network connecting the DLPFC to the subgenual anterior cingulate cortex and amygdala.

## Market Size / Affected Population
WHO estimates ~280 million people globally live with depression. US: ~31.4 million adults (18–65) reported MDD in 2023, ~30% (~3 million adults) treatment-resistant — the core addressable population. Global TMS device market valued ~$1.7–1.9 billion (2024/2025), projected to $2.2–3.2 billion by 2030 (estimates vary by research house).

## Technology
Typical NeuroStar course: 36 sessions over ~6 weeks (5 sessions/week for 6 weeks, then a 3-week taper). Each session 19–37 minutes, in-office, no anesthesia, patient resumes normal activity immediately. Real-world outcomes (TrakStar registry): 83% of completers experience measurable relief, 62% achieve remission.

## Novelty / Differentiation
First TMS device ever FDA-cleared (2008) and the largest installed base of any TMS company. Leverages its TrakStar real-world-data registry to win expanded FDA indications (adolescent clearance substantially supported by real-world data on 1,169 adolescents rather than solely a new pivotal RCT). December 2024 Greenbrook acquisition shifted the company toward vertically integrated clinic ownership/operation vs. BrainsWay's device-only model.

## Limitations — Practical
Substantial treatment burden — daily in-clinic visits for ~6 weeks; self-pay cost of a full course typically $8,000–$12,000 ($250–$600/session); insurance (including Medicare Part B) now widely covers TMS but copays/coinsurance and prior-authorization hurdles remain. Requires capital equipment and trained staff at each clinic site, limiting geographic access, especially rural areas.

## Limitations — Theoretical / Readiness
Reliance on real-world registry data (rather than new randomized trials) for some expanded indications draws scientific scrutiny regarding rigor vs. traditional RCT-based clearance. Individual response is variable; remission rates leave a substantial non-responder population. Bipolar depression indication remains only at Breakthrough Device Designation (granted March 6, 2020) — not yet fully cleared.

## Regulatory & Clinical Status
First FDA clearance 2008 (510(k), first-line/adjunct MDD after failure of ≥1 antidepressant); expanded MDD indication 2017; adjunctive OCD clearance 2022; anxious depression clearance July 19, 2022; adolescent MDD (ages 15–21) adjunct clearance March 25, 2024. FDA Breakthrough Device Designation for bipolar depression granted March 6, 2020 (not yet a full clearance). Key trial: O'Reardon et al. 2007 pivotal RCT (n=301).
