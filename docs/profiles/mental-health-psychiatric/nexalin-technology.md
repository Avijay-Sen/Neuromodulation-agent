## Target Nerve or Mechanism

**Delivery Mechanism:** "Deep Intracranial Frequency Stimulation" (DIFS) — a transcranial alternating-current (tACS-type) stimulation applied via scalp electrodes, using a proprietary "dynamic frequency" waveform intended to reach deep midbrain structures implicated in mood, sleep, and cognition without surgery or drugs. The Gen-3 "HALO Clarity" device operates at 15 mA ([BioSpace](https://www.biospace.com/nexalin-technology-unveils-next-generation-halo-clarity)). A patent describes the dynamic-frequency method ([USPTO 11,944,806](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11944806)).
**Biological basis:** tACS is proposed to modulate calcium-dependent presynaptic neurotransmitter release and cortical oscillations linked to mood/sleep circuits — see mechanism review: "Mechanisms and clinical applications of transcranial alternating current stimulation in the treatment of neuropsychiatric disorders" ([PMC13043265](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13043265/), 2025). A GRADE-assessed systematic review/meta-analysis found tACS improves subjective sleep quality but **not** objective sleep efficiency, with overall low-certainty evidence: "Efficacy of transcranial alternating current stimulation for insomnia" (*Sleep Science and Practice*, 2025) ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s41606-025-00163-6)). Nexalin's own Gen-2 China trial (Xuanwu Hospital / Beijing Anding Hospital, n=120 chronic insomnia patients) is reported by the company as published in the *Journal of Psychiatric Research* ([BioSpace](https://www.biospace.com/nexalin-technology-announces-positive-results-from-clinical-study-of-its-gen-2-tacs-device-for-treating-adult-patients-with-chronic-insomnia)) — the underlying journal article itself could not be independently located, so this published-study claim is company-reported and **[unverified]** pending direct confirmation.

## Regulatory Status & Clinical Trial

- **No FDA clearance or approval currently exists for the Gen-2/Gen-3 HALO Clarity device in the US.** The company is pursuing a planned **De Novo Classification Request** — a different, non-Breakthrough regulatory pathway — supported by a not-yet-enrolled pivotal trial with enrollment targeted for Q2 2026 ([BioSpace](https://www.biospace.com/press-releases/nexalin-technology-advances-pivotal-clinical-trial-of-halo-clarity-toward-q2-2026-enrollment)). **No FDA Breakthrough Device Designation was found** for HALO Clarity in this research pass.
- The planned US pivotal insomnia trial is described as a 160-participant randomized, triple-blind, sham-controlled study; **no NCT number could be located** — it does not appear yet registered on ClinicalTrials.gov as of this research pass.
- An earlier, different device application has a registered trial: [NCT03540745](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03540745), "Nexalin Therapy as a Viable Adjunctive Treatment for Substance Use Disorders" — this is a separate indication from the current HALO Clarity insomnia/anxiety/depression program.
- Gen-2 device is reported as cleared/approved for insomnia in China (NMPA), and separately approved in Brazil and Oman per company statements (secondary source, direct fetch blocked) — **[unverified]** against a primary regulator source.
- Brazil IRB approval for a new anxiety/insomnia trial was announced April 24, 2025 ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2025/04/24/3067474/0/en/Nexalin-Technology-Receives-IRB-Approval-in-Brazil-and-Begins-Clinical-Trial-for-Anxiety-and-Insomnia.html)).
- The company's original Gen-1 device is reported to have held an older FDA clearance (aggregator sources reference a 2003-era clearance), with new US sales of Gen-1 later suspended following FDA reclassification — this could not be independently confirmed against a primary FDA source in this pass — **[unverified]**.

## Novelty & Differentiation

Positions itself as a non-invasive, drug-free alternative to pharmacotherapy for anxiety, depression, and insomnia, distinct from competing at-home neuromodulation devices such as the Fisher Wallace Stimulator (cranial electrotherapy stimulation) and Flow Neuroscience (tDCS headset for depression). Nexalin's claimed differentiator is deeper brain penetration via a proprietary "dynamic frequency" tACS waveform versus fixed-frequency tDCS/CES devices.

---

## Limitations: Practical & Theoretical

- No US FDA clearance/approval for the current-generation device; US commercialization is contingent on a not-yet-completed De Novo pathway.
- The broader tACS evidence base for anxiety/depression/insomnia is characterized in independent reviews as low-certainty and heterogeneous ([PMC13043265](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13043265/)).
- Company's own key efficacy claim (Gen-2 China insomnia study) rests on a company-reported publication that could not be independently located in this pass — **[unverified]**.
- Recurring capital raises, including an August 2026 short-term liquidity note, suggest financial fragility risk for a small/micro-cap public company.
