# Flow Neuroscience AB

**Category:** Mental Health / Psychiatric (MDD, moderate-to-severe, non-treatment-refractory) | **Status:** Private | **HQ:** Malmö, Sweden | **Founded:** 2016

## Funding & Company Status
$9.3 million Series A (August 2021), led by Khosla Ventures with CSS Group, Zühlke Ventures, Kirin Holdings, SOSV's HAX, and Schox. Total lifetime funding figures conflict across sources — PitchBook reports ~$12.6M, other aggregators report $22–26M across ~4 rounds including an April 2025 round — treat as approximate, roughly $10–26M range, unable to fully reconcile. FY2024 revenue reported only as a broad, low-confidence $5–25M range. ~16 employees as of mid-2024. Remains private.

## Target Nerve / Mechanism
Transcranial direct current stimulation (tDCS) via a two-electrode montage (anode typically over the left dorsolateral prefrontal cortex, cathode over a contralateral/supraorbital reference site), delivering a constant low-intensity (~2 mA) direct current for ~30 minutes/session, self-administered at home via the FL-100 headset and companion smartphone app that also delivers behavioral-activation content.

## Biological Basis
Nitsche & Paulus, 2000, *The Journal of Physiology*, and Nitsche & Paulus, 2001, *Neurology* 57(10):1899–1901 — anodal stimulation depolarizes/increases cortical excitability while cathodal hyperpolarizes/decreases it, with longer-lasting effects implicating NMDA-receptor-dependent plasticity. Flow applies this to the same left-DLPFC-hypoactivity model of depression used to justify TMS, aiming to non-invasively raise excitability in the underactive region.

## Market Size / Affected Population
Same global depression burden as above (WHO ~280M). Flow's FDA-cleared population (moderate-to-severe MDD, **non-treatment-refractory**) is broader/earlier-line than TMS/Deep TMS's treatment-resistant niche, since it doesn't require a prior medication failure — a potentially larger addressable population, though also more direct competition with first-line antidepressants and psychotherapy. Press reporting cites a planned US retail price point around $500 for the headset.

## Technology
FL-100 device (headset + reusable electrodes/pads + companion app), prescription-required in the US; pivotal EMPOWER trial protocol used a 10-week course of near-daily ~30-minute home sessions, remotely monitored, with app-guided behavioral-activation therapy delivered alongside stimulation.

## Novelty / Differentiation
The first and only FDA-authorized at-home neuromodulation device for depression in the US, and the first CE-marked at-home tDCS treatment used within the UK NHS (since 2019 EU launch); claims over 55,000 users internationally (UK, EU, Brazil, Australia, UAE). The most fundamentally different care model in this category — no clinic visits at all, vs. Neuronetics/BrainsWay's in-office-only models.

## Limitations — Practical
Self-administration means less clinician oversight per session — electrode placement consistency and compliance depend on the patient/caregiver. US commercial launch was only expected around mid-2026 following the December 2025 FDA action, so US insurance coverage/reimbursement pathway is not yet established. Device + recurring pad costs still represent an out-of-pocket barrier for uninsured patients, though far below a full TMS course.

## Limitations — Theoretical / Readiness
tDCS's mechanism of action, while grounded in the Nitsche/Paulus work, remains considerably more contested in the broader literature than TMS's — multiple meta-analyses (2024 systematic review, PMC11258305; 2025 electric-field-modeling meta-analysis) report heterogeneous, sometimes null results across the wider tDCS trial literature, raising explicit concern about placebo/expectancy confounds as a live methodological issue for the category, even though Flow's own EMPOWER trial was itself sham-controlled and statistically positive. Long-term durability beyond the 10-week primary endpoint is not yet established. At-home consumer neurostimulation has historically drawn FDA/scientific skepticism due to unregulated "wellness" tDCS devices sold direct-to-consumer, which may create market confusion around Flow's now-cleared product.

## Regulatory & Clinical Status
CE Mark obtained 2019 (EU, enabling at-home use and NHS pilots in parts of the UK). US FDA: Premarket Approval (PMA P230024), approved December 8, 2025 — note: numerous press outlets loosely characterized this as a "De Novo"/"first-of-its-kind clearance," but the underlying FDA record identifies it specifically as a **PMA** approval (a distinction worth flagging for diligence precision). Indication: adults 18+ with moderate-to-severe MDD in the current episode, monotherapy or adjunct, not treatment-refractory. Pivotal trial: EMPOWER (NCT05202119), published as Woodham et al., 2025, "Home-based transcranial direct current stimulation treatment for major depressive disorder: a fully remote phase 2 randomized sham-controlled trial," *Nature Medicine* 31:87–95 — fully remote, 2-center (US/UK), double-blind, placebo-controlled RCT of 174 patients (87 active/87 sham); active tDCS produced 2–3x higher remission/response rates than sham, 58% remission by week 10, symptom reduction detectable within 3 weeks, mild/transient side effects. US commercial availability expected mid-2026.
