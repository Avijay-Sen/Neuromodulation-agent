# Nyxoah SA

**Category:** Sleep Apnea | **Ticker:** Euronext Brussels/Paris & NASDAQ: NYXH | **HQ:** Mont-Saint-Guibert, Belgium | **Founded:** 2009

## Funding & Company Status
Founded in Israel by Robert Taub. Euronext Brussels IPO September 2020 raised €85M; NASDAQ IPO followed July 2021. Total equity raised since inception: €332.5M. Additional raises: €69.7M (2024), €21.9M (2025); ~$95M underwritten offering plus $15M EIB loan tranche in June 2026 (~$110M that month). Cash and equivalents €25.9M as of March 31, 2026. Market cap mid-2026 ~€141.9M (stock ~€1.40–1.50), down sharply from its 52-week high (~€8.87).

## Target Nerve / Mechanism
Hypoglossal nerve, **bilateral** stimulation (both branches, vs. Inspire's unilateral approach). Delivered via a small, leadless, battery-free implant placed under the chin/floor of mouth through a single incision; powered and controlled wirelessly by an external wearable device worn during sleep.

## Biological Basis
Same core OSA pathophysiology as hypoglossal-targeted therapies generally (loss of genioglossus/pharyngeal dilator tone during sleep permitting airway collapse — see Oliven et al. 2003, https://journals.physiology.org/doi/full/10.1152/japplphysiol.00203.2003). Nyxoah's differentiator rests on the hypothesis that bilateral stimulation produces more consistent tongue-base advancement across sleep positions, including supine sleep, than unilateral stimulation. Independent, non-company-sponsored peer-reviewed confirmation of a bilateral-vs-unilateral outcome advantage was not found; the supporting evidence currently derives primarily from Nyxoah's own DREAM/BETTER SLEEP trials (Journal of Clinical Sleep Medicine, 2025) — an area flagged for further diligence.

## Market Size / Affected Population
Same global prevalence base as above (936M mild-to-severe OSA, 425M moderate-to-severe; Benjafield et al. 2019). Nyxoah's FDA-approved population is moderate-to-severe OSA adults with AHI 15–65 who have failed/refused/not tolerated CPAP. The company separately pursues patients with complete concentric collapse (CCC) of the soft palate — excluded from standard Inspire eligibility, an estimated 20–30% of OSA patients on DISE evaluation — via its ACCCESS pivotal study (FDA Breakthrough Device Designation granted Sept 14, 2021; reported meeting primary safety/efficacy endpoints per a September 2026 press release). No specific dollar addressable-market figure was found disclosed by the company.

## Technology
(1) A small leadless implant (no internal battery) placed submentally through a single incision; (2) each night, the patient positions an external wearable "Activation Chip"/patch against the skin over the implant; (3) the wearable inductively powers the implant and transmits stimulation parameters wirelessly (no implanted battery to replace surgically); (4) delivers bilateral stimulation to both hypoglossal nerve branches; (5) unlike Inspire, Genio has no implanted respiratory-sensing lead — stimulation is open-loop/duty-cycle based, not breath-synchronized; (6) compatible with 1.5T and 3T full-body MRI due to the absence of a battery/pulse generator.

## Novelty / Differentiation
Key differentiators vs. Inspire: (1) bilateral vs. unilateral stimulation; (2) leadless, battery-free implant (no chest incision, single-incision placement) vs. Inspire's chest-implanted IPG; (3) broader MRI compatibility; (4) explicit pursuit of the complete-concentric-collapse sub-population Inspire's standard therapy excludes, via the Breakthrough-Designated ACCCESS program. Inspire filed a patent infringement lawsuit against Nyxoah in May 2025 alleging Genio infringes three Inspire patents — an active litigation risk.

## Limitations — Practical
Still requires surgical implantation (single incision, but a device implant with surgical risk). Patients must wear and correctly position an external activation device nightly — a nightly compliance/positioning burden distinct from Inspire's simple remote-activation model. The DREAM pivotal trial (115 patients) reported an 8.7% serious adverse event rate (11 SAEs in 10 subjects, including 3 device-related events and 3 explants). US commercial/reimbursement infrastructure is nascent (FDA approval only as of August 2025).

## Limitations — Theoretical / Readiness
As a new US entrant, Genio lacks anything comparable to Inspire's decade-plus, 100,000+ patient real-world experience — durability of the wireless-power/leadless architecture and multi-year outcome durability remain largely unproven outside clinical trial cohorts (largest reported: DREAM's 115 patients at 12 months). The case for bilateral being categorically superior to unilateral is still primarily supported by company-sponsored trial data. The ongoing Inspire patent lawsuit is a business/legal readiness risk independent of the clinical technology.

## Regulatory & Clinical Status
CE Mark obtained earlier (exact date unable to verify). FDA Breakthrough Device Designation granted September 14, 2021 for OSA with complete concentric collapse. FDA PMA approval (P240024) granted August 8, 2025, for moderate-to-severe OSA, AHI 15–65. Key trials: **DREAM** (NCT03868618) — 115 patients, US/Australia/Belgium/Germany; met co-primary endpoints, 63.5% ITT AHI responder rate (p=0.002), median 70.8% AHI reduction at 12 months. **BETTER SLEEP** — multicenter open-label trial, statistically significant AHI reduction at 6 months in both whole cohort and CCC subgroup. **ACCCESS** — IDE-approved pivotal study in CCC patients, reported meeting primary safety/efficacy endpoints (September 2026 press release), positioning for potential label expansion.
