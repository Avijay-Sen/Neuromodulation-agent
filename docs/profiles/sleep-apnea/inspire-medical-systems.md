# Inspire Medical Systems, Inc.

**Category:** Sleep Apnea | **Ticker:** NYSE: INSP | **HQ:** Maple Grove, Minnesota, USA | **Founded:** 2007

## Funding & Company Status
Raised ~$109M in private venture funding pre-IPO (Kleiner Perkins Caufield & Byers, U.S. Venture Partners, OrbiMed, Synergy Life Science Partners, Amzak Capital, plus strategic investment from Medtronic and Johnson & Johnson in the Series F round). IPO on NYSE completed May 7, 2018, raising ~$112M net. Market cap ~$1.58B (May 2026) and ~$1.22B (June 2026) — decline reflects a stock pullback tied to U.S. reimbursement/coding disruption. FY2026 revenue guidance was cut mid-year from $950M–$1.0B to $835M–$875M; Q2 2026 revenue was $200.6M (down 7.6% YoY).

## Target Nerve / Mechanism
Hypoglossal nerve (unilateral stimulation). A fully implanted system senses the respiratory cycle via a chest sensing lead and delivers synchronized electrical stimulation to the hypoglossal nerve (innervating the genioglossus and other tongue muscles) during inspiration, causing the tongue to move forward/stiffen and keep the airway open.

## Biological Basis
OSA arises from repetitive collapse of the pharyngeal airway during sleep, driven by anatomical narrowing and loss of upper-airway dilator muscle tone. The genioglossus is the principal pharyngeal dilator muscle, contracting phasically with each inspiration; at sleep onset, neural drive to this muscle falls, and in anatomically predisposed individuals the airway narrows or collapses (Physiological Society review, "Control of upper airway muscles and the pathophysiology of obstructive sleep apnoea"). Electrical activation of the genioglossus via the hypoglossal nerve increases airway caliber and reduces collapsibility (Oliven et al., *Journal of Applied Physiology*, 2003, https://journals.physiology.org/doi/full/10.1152/japplphysiol.00203.2003). The pivotal STAR trial (Strollo et al., *NEJM*, 2014; PMID 24401051) demonstrated this translates into clinically meaningful AHI reduction in humans.

## Market Size / Affected Population
Globally, ~936 million adults aged 30–69 have mild-to-severe OSA and ~425 million have moderate-to-severe OSA (Benjafield et al., *The Lancet Respiratory Medicine*, 2019, https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lanres/article/PIIS2213-2600(19)30198-5/abstract). CPAP is first-line therapy, but adherence is poor: ~34% non-adherence (7hr/night threshold), flat over 20 years (PMC4992257). Inspire's addressable population is CPAP-failed/intolerant adults with moderate-to-severe OSA (AHI 15–100 after 2023 label expansion) and BMI ≤40, without complete concentric palatal collapse. Third-party vendor estimates size the hypoglossal nerve stimulation device market at ~$0.45–1.2B (2024) growing to $1.2B–$5.9B by 2033–2034 (estimates vary widely — directional only). Inspire has surpassed 100,000 patients implanted worldwide as of May 2025.

## Technology
(1) A neurostimulator ("IPG") is implanted subcutaneously in the upper chest; (2) a sensing lead between intercostal muscles detects breathing pattern; (3) a stimulation lead is cuffed around a hypoglossal nerve branch under the jaw; (4) patient activates nightly via handheld remote; (5) once asleep, the IPG times electrical pulses to each inspiratory effort; (6) newest generation (Inspire V, full US launch May 2025) adds Bluetooth connectivity and expanded MRI conditional labeling.

## Novelty / Differentiation
First (and for a decade the only) FDA-approved implantable neurostimulation therapy for OSA; market leader with the largest long-term evidence base (STAR trial + ADHERE registry, 5,000-patient target enrollment reached 2024). Senses respiration directly via an implanted lead, delivering unilateral, closed-loop, breath-synchronized stimulation (vs. Nyxoah's open-loop bilateral approach), plus a much larger installed base and multi-year outcome data.

## Limitations — Practical
Outpatient surgical implantation with associated risk; total procedure cost estimated $30,000–$65,000. Battery/pulse-generator replacement is a future minor surgery (~$5,000–$8,000 estimated). Strict patient-selection criteria: age ≥22, moderate-to-severe OSA, documented CPAP failure/intolerance, BMI ≤40 (many payers apply stricter cutoffs), and absence of complete concentric palatal collapse on DISE (a pattern in an estimated 20–30% of OSA patients) — a contraindication for standard therapy.

## Limitations — Theoretical / Readiness
DISE-based exclusion of concentric collapse is an imperfect predictor of non-response; predicting HGNS efficacy from collapse patterns is still active research. Long-term data beyond ~5–10 years (device longevity, lead durability) are still accumulating via the ADHERE registry (enrollment complete 2024, follow-up complete 2025, full analysis pending). Reimbursement/coding infrastructure has proven fragile in practice — Inspire itself cited U.S. reimbursement/coding challenges as a driver of its 2026 revenue guidance cut, a decade post-approval. Trained-implanter/DISE-capable sleep-center capacity may constrain scale-up in underserved geographies.

## Regulatory & Clinical Status
Original FDA PMA approval April 2014 (P130008) for moderate-to-severe OSA, AHI 20–65, BMI ≤32, CPAP failure/intolerance. Supplemental approvals (March/June 2023, P130008/S089, S090) raised BMI ceiling to 40 and broadened AHI range to 100. Pivotal STAR trial (Strollo et al., NEJM 2014, PMID 24401051) — 126 subjects, all endpoints met; 5-year follow-up showed median AHI reduced from 29.3 to 9.0 events/hr at 12 months and 6.2 at 5 years, 80% still using therapy nightly. ADHERE real-world registry (target 5,000 patients) fully enrolled 2024, follow-up complete 2025, full results pending.
