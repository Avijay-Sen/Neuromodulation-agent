## Clinical Indication

**Disorder Category:** Sleep Apnea
**Primary Indication:** Obstructive sleep apnea (OSA)

---

## Target Nerve or Mechanism

**Anatomical Target:** Hypoglossal nerve and ansa cervicalis nerve (dual-nerve targeting)
**Delivery Mechanism:** Percutaneous placement of small electrode arrays near both the hypoglossal nerve and the ansa cervicalis nerve — described as an injectable neurostimulator delivered via a ~5–10 minute outpatient procedure without a surgical incision ([Tracxn](https://tracxn.com/d/companies/invicta-medical); [Consultant Surgeon](https://www.consultant-surgeon.co.uk/osaimplantrestera)).
**Biological basis:** Kent, Zealear & Schwartz, "Ansa Cervicalis and Hypoglossal Nerve Stimulation in a Patient With Obstructive Sleep Apnea," *Otolaryngology–Head and Neck Surgery*, 2021 — combined ansa cervicalis + hypoglossal stimulation increased retropalatal cross-sectional area 4.3-fold versus 2.5-fold for hypoglossal stimulation alone ([PubMed 33494663](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33494663/)). Follow-up mechanistic work: "Ansa cervicalis stimulation increases pharyngeal patency in patients with obstructive sleep apnea," *J Appl Physiol*, 2021 ([full text](https://journals.physiology.org/doi/full/10.1152/japplphysiol.00076.2021)).

## Regulatory Status & Clinical Trial

- Two Invicta Medical-sponsored trials appear on ClinicalTrials.gov: **[NCT06283030](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06283030)** ("Acute Proof-of-Concept Study to Evaluate Hypoglossal Nerve Stimulation in Humans With Obstructive Sleep Apnea," sponsor org ID IM-001, status Completed, start Jan 14, 2022, primary completion May 31, 2023, n=14) and **[NCT06283017](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06283017)** (a companion study).
- A related but Vanderbilt-sponsored (not Invicta-sponsored) ansa cervicalis study is [NCT05501236](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05501236), with named investigator Alan Schwartz having consultant ties to Invicta.
- **No FDA clearance, IDE approval, or Breakthrough Device Designation for Invicta/Restera could be found** via this research pass — **[unverified]**. The company states its device has been implanted in 63 patients worldwide and targets a 2027 commercial launch, but this is company-sourced, not FDA-verified ([Consultant Surgeon](https://www.consultant-surgeon.co.uk/osaimplantrestera)).

## Novelty & Differentiation

Positioned by the company as a "third generation" hypoglossal nerve stimulation approach — percutaneous/injectable rather than a surgical implant (Inspire requires an incision and a battery pocket) — and dual-nerve targeting (hypoglossal + ansa cervicalis) versus the single-nerve approaches used by Inspire, ImThera/LivaNova, and Nyxoah. This positioning is drawn from general synthesis of competitor-landscape coverage rather than a single citable comparative source — **[unverified]** as an independently-verified competitive claim.

## Limitations: Practical & Theoretical

- Only a small (n=14) proof-of-concept trial has been completed; no randomized/controlled efficacy data were found.
- No confirmed FDA pathway or milestone was identified in this research pass.
- Durability of an injected (non-anchored) electrode over the long term is unproven — this is an analytical/reasoning-based limitation rather than one drawn from a specific citation.

## Market Size

Global OSA prevalence is estimated at **936 million adults** (mild-to-severe) and **425 million** (moderate-to-severe), aged 30–69, per Benjafield et al., *Lancet Respiratory Medicine*, 2019 ([The Lancet](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lanres/article/PIIS2213-2600(19)30198-5/abstract); [ResMed summary](https://newsroom.resmed.com/news-releases/news-details/2019/The-Lancet-Publishes-More-Than-936-Million-Have-Obstructive-Sleep-Apnea-Worldwide/default.aspx)).
