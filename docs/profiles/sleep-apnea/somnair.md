# Somnair Inc.

## Company Profile

**Company Name:** Somnair Inc.
**HQ Location:** Baltimore, MD, USA ([EngageTU](https://www.engagetu.com/2025/03/12/medstar-health-supports-somnair-as-startup-accelerators-first-medstar-health-innovation-venture/))

The project originated as a CBID master's design project (originally named "SomnOSA") around 2022–2023; exact incorporation date could not be verified — **[unverified]** ([JHU CBID](https://cbid.bme.jhu.edu/2025/09/07/johns-hopkins-alumnis-somnair-raises-4-3m-to-develop-sleep-apnea-device/); [JHU BME project gallery](https://www.bme.jhu.edu/academics/bme-design/bme-project-gallery/a-novel-neurostimulation-solution-for-a-highly-prevalent-poorly-treated-form-of-obstructive-sleep-apnea/))

No prior institutional funding rounds were found; the company likely relied on grant/accelerator support pre-seed, including the MedStar Health StarTUp Accelerator and the Fogarty Innovation accelerator program ([Fogarty Innovation](https://www.fogartyinnovation.org/a-better-nights-sleep-somnair-joins-fogarty-innovations-company-accelerator-program/)).

## Clinical Indication

**Disorder Category:** Sleep Apnea
**Primary Indication:** Obstructive sleep apnea (OSA)

---

## Target Nerve or Mechanism

**Delivery Mechanism:** A retainer-like intraoral device worn at bedtime, containing a circuit that delivers non-invasive electrical stimulation "similar to a muscle stimulator" to a target nerve/muscle controlling palate and throat muscle activity, opening the airway ([JHU Ventures](https://ventures.jhu.edu/news/a-better-less-invasive-treatment-for-sleep-apnea/)). The exact nerve target was not explicitly confirmed in available sources beyond this general description — **[unverified]** precise nerve target.
**Biological basis:** General hypoglossal nerve/genioglossus-activation literature underpins the airway-opening rationale (see the Invicta Medical profile for the core mechanism papers). A directly analogous non-invasive intraoral approach is described in "A novel intraoral neuromuscular stimulation device for treating sleep-disordered breathing," which describes transmucosal stimulation of the hypoglossal nerve/genioglossus and palatoglossus muscle via an oral appliance — relevant supporting mechanism literature, though not confirmed as Somnair's own study ([PMC8590646](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8590646/)).

## Regulatory Status & Clinical Trial

- A proof-of-concept human study is underway at Johns Hopkins Hospital with physicians Luu Van Pham and Kevin M. Motz; early results in **22 patients** are reported to support the airway-opening hypothesis ([JHU Ventures](https://ventures.jhu.edu/news/a-better-less-invasive-treatment-for-sleep-apnea/)).
- The company states an intent to achieve **FDA clearance by end of 2027** — a company-stated target, not a confirmed regulatory milestone.
- **No FDA clearance, IDE, Breakthrough Device Designation, or registered ClinicalTrials.gov NCT number for Somnair could be found** — **[unverified]**.
- **Note on a name collision:** "Bairitone Health's Somnar" is an unrelated sonar-sensor OSA *diagnostic* company with a real FDA Breakthrough Device Designation ([AASM](https://aasm.org/bairitone-health-receives-fda-breakthrough-device-designation-for-somnar/)) — this is a different company from Somnair Inc. and should not be confused with it.

---

## Novelty & Differentiation

Marketed as the "first non-invasive neurostimulation" treatment for OSA, contrasted with surgical implants like Inspire Medical, LivaNova/ImThera, and Nyxoah, which all require surgical implantation of a pulse generator and cuff electrode. Somnair's device requires no surgery, is removable, and is used only during sleep like an oral appliance ([JHU Ventures](https://ventures.jhu.edu/news/a-better-less-invasive-treatment-for-sleep-apnea/); [JHU CBID](https://cbid.bme.jhu.edu/2025/09/07/johns-hopkins-alumnis-somnair-raises-4-3m-to-develop-sleep-apnea-device/)) — this framing has not been independently verified with a head-to-head comparative source.

---

## Limitations: Practical & Theoretical

- Very early stage — a small (n=22), non-randomized proof-of-concept study, not yet a peer-reviewed publication of the company's own trial data.
- Non-invasive transmucosal stimulation has historically shown weaker/less durable effect sizes than implanted hypoglossal nerve stimulation in the broader literature — a general inference, not a Somnair-specific finding.
- FDA pathway and the stated 2027 clearance timeline are company-stated and externally unverified.
