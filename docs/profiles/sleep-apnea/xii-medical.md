## Clinical Indication

**Disorder Category:** Sleep Apnea
**Primary Indication:** Obstructive sleep apnea (OSA)

---

## Target Nerve or Mechanism

**Anatomical Target:** Hypoglossal nerve (CN XII)
**Delivery Mechanism:** Electrical stimulation of the hypoglossal nerve contracts the genioglossus muscle, protruding the tongue and preventing pharyngeal collapse during sleep — the same general mechanism-of-action class as Inspire Medical, LivaNova/ImThera, and Nyxoah ([MassDevice](https://www.massdevice.com/xii-medical-closes-45m-series-b/)).
**Biological basis:** Foundational physiology and clinical evidence for hypoglossal nerve/genioglossus stimulation restoring upper-airway patency comes from Strollo et al., "Upper-Airway Stimulation for Obstructive Sleep Apnea" (the STAR trial), *NEJM*, 2014 ([PubMed 24401051](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24401051/); [full text](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1308659)), and mechanistic work by Oliven et al., "Upper airway response to electrical stimulation of the genioglossus in obstructive sleep apnea," *J Appl Physiol*, 2003 ([full text](https://journals.physiology.org/doi/full/10.1152/japplphysiol.00203.2003)).

## Novelty & Differentiation

The company describes a "best-in-class" neuromodulation platform aimed at improving patient compliance and reducing surgical burden versus incumbents, offering a smaller, more flexible, easier-to-implant device with a less invasive surgical approach than current hypoglossal nerve stimulators ([MassDevice](https://www.massdevice.com/xii-medical-closes-45m-series-b/)). Competitive landscape includes: Inspire Medical (unilateral, breath-synchronized, FDA-approved 2014, 100,000+ implants; [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/recently-approved-devices/inspire-upper-airway-stimulation-p130008s090)); Nyxoah's Genio (bilateral, battery-free, externally powered, open-loop, FDA-approved 2025); LivaNova/ImThera's aura6000 (unilateral, stimulates the main hypoglossal trunk, FDA-approved 2026); and Invicta Medical, running an early feasibility study ([NCT06283017](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06283017)).

## Limitations: Practical & Theoretical

- Hypoglossal nerve stimulation as a device class has highly selective patient eligibility — in the STAR trial, only 126 of 929 screened patients (13.6%) were ultimately implanted ([PubMed 24401051](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24401051/)).
- Efficacy is phenotype-dependent: patients with concentric/complete palatal collapse on drug-induced sleep endoscopy respond poorly to this device class.
- Class-wide adverse events include implant-site pain, tongue weakness/abrasion, and device malfunction ([systematic review, PMC11313308](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11313308/)).
- XII Medical-specific trial safety/efficacy data could not be verified as publicly reported — **[unverified]**.
