# Signifier Medical Technologies (eXciteOSA)

**Category:** Sleep Apnea | **Status:** Private | **HQ:** London, UK (additional offices Needham, MA and Berlin, Germany) | **Founded:** 2015 (as Snoozeal Limited)

## Funding & Company Status
Total disclosed venture funding ~$57–58M across at least four priced rounds (Crunchbase): >$13M by June 2019, $10M Series C (Sept 2020, led by Alan Howard), $35M Series D (July 2021, to accelerate eXciteOSA commercialization). Separately, in December 2024, existing investors (Waha Capital PJSC, Angelus Sano Fund L.P., Segulah Medical Acceleration AB) purchased ~$49M of existing shares — a secondary transaction, not confirmed new primary funding. Remains private; no public market cap.

## Target Nerve / Mechanism
Intrinsic/extrinsic tongue muscles, including the genioglossus, via direct transcutaneous neuromuscular electrical stimulation (NMES) — **not** nerve stimulation in the implantable sense. Surface stimulation delivered through a mouthpiece during a supervised daytime session, intended to produce a training/strengthening effect on tongue muscle tone over a multi-week course. Key distinction from Inspire/Nyxoah, which stimulate the hypoglossal nerve during sleep.

## Biological Basis
Target pathology is the same loss of pharyngeal dilator muscle tone (genioglossus) permitting airway collapse during sleep. Signifier's mechanistic bet is a "muscle-training" hypothesis: daytime, awake-state NMES of the tongue improves muscle tone/endurance/mobility such that the trained muscle better resists collapse passively during sleep — an indirect, resistance-training-like mechanism rather than real-time nerve stimulation during sleep. Pivotal study: Baptista et al., *Journal of Clinical Medicine*, 2021, 10(9) (125 enrolled, 115 completed, 92% completion). Second source: Nokes et al., *Sleep & Breathing*, 2022. A sham-controlled RCT (**AEGIS**) was reported as "in press" at *ERJ Open Research*; final published results not independently confirmed in this research.

## Market Size / Affected Population
Same global OSA prevalence base (936M mild-to-severe), but eXciteOSA's FDA-cleared population is narrower and different in kind: primary snorers and adults with **mild** OSA only (AHI <15), ages 18+ — patients who would not qualify for Inspire or Genio (both restricted to moderate-to-severe OSA). No independently verified TAM figure specific to eXciteOSA found. Company disclosed a 10,000-unit order from HME distributor Spectrum Sleep Solutions (June 2024). CMS established HCPCS billing codes effective October 1, 2023 (priced January 10, 2024), but many commercial payers had not established medical-necessity coverage; cash-pay list price ~$104/month.

## Technology
(1) Mouthpiece with four electrodes (two above, two below the tongue); (2) delivers NMES (TENS-like) directly to tongue muscles including genioglossus; (3) protocol: 20 minutes/day for an initial 6-week course, then ongoing weekly maintenance; (4) used while **awake during the day**, not during sleep; (5) a 2025 hardware update added a remote control to meet CMS coding/reimbursement documentation requirements.

## Novelty / Differentiation
Only FDA-authorized device in this trio that is entirely non-invasive/non-implantable and used during the daytime — received FDA De Novo authorization (DEN200018, February 5, 2021) as a novel device type (21 CFR 872.5575) with no prior predicate. Structurally non-competitive with Inspire/Nyxoah for its approved population (mild OSA/snoring vs. their moderate-to-severe), positioning it as a potential adjunct/entry-point therapy. Company-sponsored data claims >80% adherence (not independently audited).

## Limitations — Practical
Requires an initial 6-week course of daily 20-minute sessions plus indefinite weekly maintenance — higher ongoing compliance burden than a "set and forget" implant, and efficacy is contingent on continued use. Original FDA clearance carried dental/oral contraindications only removed via a February 2023 supplemental clearance. Reimbursement is still immature, leaving a substantial cash-pay component. Not indicated for moderate/severe OSA — cannot serve the larger, more symptomatic segment implants target.

## Limitations — Theoretical / Readiness
The "muscle training" mechanism is less direct than real-time nerve stimulation during sleep; peer-reviewed evidence base, while real, is comparatively thinner and drawn from smaller/shorter studies than Inspire's STAR/ADHERE program. Long-term durability of the muscle-training effect, and whether benefit persists if maintenance sessions lapse, not addressed with long-term follow-up data found in this research. As a fully private company, transparency into aggregate real-world outcomes/safety data is more limited than for the two public/FDA-PMA-tracked companies. Broader ecosystem readiness (payer policy formation, primary-care/dental awareness) also appears less mature.

## Regulatory & Clinical Status
FDA De Novo classification granted February 5, 2021 (DEN200018) — Class II neuromuscular tongue stimulator (21 CFR 872.5575), for snoring and mild OSA (AHI <15), ages 18+; first FDA marketing authorization of its kind. 510(k) supplement in 2023 (K223446) added a remote-control variant. February 2023 label change removed dental contraindications. Peer-reviewed support: Baptista et al. 2021; Nokes et al. 2022. Registered trial NCT05183009 identified; detailed results not independently verified. AEGIS sham-controlled RCT reported forthcoming/in-press in ERJ Open Research; final results not independently confirmed.
