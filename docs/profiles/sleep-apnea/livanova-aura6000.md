# LivaNova aura6000 (originally ImThera Medical)

## Company Profile

**Company Name:** LivaNova PLC (device originally developed by ImThera Medical, Inc., acquired 2018)
**HQ Location:** London, UK (LivaNova plc corporate HQ); ImThera Medical was originally San Diego, CA, USA
**Founded:** ImThera Medical's founding year was not confirmed in this research pass — **[unverified]**. ImThera received FDA investigational device exemption for its pivotal trial in November 2014 ([MassDevice](https://www.massdevice.com/livanova-puts-225m-table-imthera-medical/)).
**Flagship product:** **aura6000 System** (proximal hypoglossal nerve stimulation)
**Website:** https://www.livanova.com

**Note on entity structure:** This profile consolidates two backlog entries ("ImThera Medical" and "LivaNova plc — aura6000 sleep apnea program") because ImThera Medical no longer operates as an independent company — LivaNova acquired it outright in 2018 and now owns the aura6000 program directly ([MassDevice](https://www.massdevice.com/livanova-closes-225m-imthera-buyout/)).

## Clinical Indication

**Disorder Category:** Sleep Apnea
**Primary Indication:** Moderate to severe Obstructive Sleep Apnea (OSA) in adults who have failed, do not tolerate, or are ineligible for first-line therapies such as CPAP — apnea-hypopnea index (AHI) 15–65 ([LivaNova investor press release](https://investor.livanova.com/news-releases/news-release-details/livanova-receives-us-food-and-drug-administration-premarket)).

---

## Target Nerve or Mechanism

**Anatomical Target:** Hypoglossal nerve (proximal trunk, targeting multiple branches via a cuff electrode)
**Delivery Mechanism:** Implanted **proximal hypoglossal nerve stimulation (p-HGNS)** — a stimulation lead placed around the proximal hypoglossal nerve trunk activates multiple tongue-protrusor muscle groups (via the genioglossus and other branches) to keep the upper airway open during sleep, paired with an implanted pulse generator near the collarbone ([FDA patient guide, P250013](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf25/P250013C.pdf)).
**Biological basis:** Hypoglossal nerve stimulation activating the genioglossus and other tongue-protrusor muscles to relieve upper-airway collapse is a peer-reviewed, clinically validated mechanism for OSA — see the randomized clinical trial of targeted hypoglossal nerve stimulation (the ImThera/THN device lineage) published in *JAMA Otolaryngology* ([PMC10080405](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10080405/)).

## Regulatory Status & Clinical Trial

- **FDA Premarket Approval (PMA)** granted **March 19, 2026** for the aura6000 System to treat moderate-to-severe OSA ([LivaNova investor press release](https://investor.livanova.com/news-releases/news-release-details/livanova-receives-us-food-and-drug-administration-premarket); [Sleep Review](https://sleepreviewmag.com/sleep-treatments/therapy-devices/neurostimulators/fda-approves-livanovas-aura6000-neurostimulator-for-osa/)).
- Approved **without a contraindication for complete concentric collapse (CCC)** at the soft palate — a patient-selection limitation that applies to Inspire's device — making aura6000 usable in a broader anatomical subgroup ([Sleep Review](https://sleepreviewmag.com/sleep-treatments/therapy-devices/neurostimulators/fda-approves-livanovas-aura6000-neurostimulator-for-osa/)).
- **OSPREY pivotal trial**: responder criteria met by **65% of patients at 12 months**; median AHI fell from **34.3 to 11.0 events/hour** ([LivaNova investor press release](https://investor.livanova.com/news-releases/news-release-details/livanova-receives-us-food-and-drug-administration-premarket)).
- Underlying THN3 randomized trial (multi-branch/targeted stimulation of the hypoglossal nerve, the mechanism aura6000 is built on) previously published in a peer-reviewed randomized clinical trial ([PMC10080405](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10080405/)).

---

## Novelty & Differentiation

- Third hypoglossal nerve stimulator to reach FDA approval in the US (after Inspire Medical Systems and Nyxoah), and — like Nyxoah — approved without Inspire's complete-concentric-collapse exclusion, but via a different mechanism: multi-branch proximal trunk stimulation rather than bilateral leadless stimulation.
- Backed by LivaNova's existing commercial/regulatory infrastructure (the company already markets VNS Therapy for epilepsy), which could accelerate US commercial rollout relative to a standalone startup.

---

## Limitations: Practical & Theoretical

- FDA approval is very recent (March 2026); **no independent long-term (multi-year) real-world outcomes or adverse-event-rate data were found in this research pass — [unverified]**.
- Surgical implant with the same general implant risks (infection, lead-related revision) common to all hypoglossal nerve stimulators.
- OSA neurostimulation device market is competitive and crowded (Inspire, Nyxoah, now LivaNova); LivaNova's aura6000-specific US commercial revenue was not found in this pass — **[unverified]**.
- ImThera's pre-acquisition history (founding year, full funding history) is thinly documented in public sources — **[unverified]**, flagged rather than guessed.

## Market Size

Obstructive sleep apnea affects an estimated **30 million US adults**, with roughly **80% undiagnosed**; the hypoglossal nerve stimulation device segment specifically is projected to exceed **$4.3 billion by 2034** (CAGR ~17.9%) ([GM Insights, US Sleep Apnea Implants Market](https://www.gminsights.com/industry-analysis/us-sleep-apnea-implants-market)).
